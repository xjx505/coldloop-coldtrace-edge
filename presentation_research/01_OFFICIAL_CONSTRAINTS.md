# Official Reboot Constraints for the Presentation

This file contains only organizer-derived constraints plus clearly labeled tactical implications.

## Deliverable

Official FAQ:

> Documentation may include a short presentation of **7–10 slides** describing the submission, including the approach and methodology.

This is the strongest hard constraint on deck length found in the organizer material.

## Judging sequence

Official September 26 agenda:
- 1:45 PM — final project submission deadline on Unite Ideas
- 2:00 PM — science-fair style judging
- 3:30 PM — final pitching and judging
- 4:30 PM — winners announced

The FAQ says judges select five finalist teams after science-fair judging, then an expert panel selects the overall winner.

### Implication

The same visual system must survive two modes:

**Science-fair mode**
- conversational;
- judges may interrupt;
- demo and artifacts can be explored;
- presenters need fast navigation to evidence.

**Final-stage mode**
- linear;
- more viewing distance;
- less time for judges to inspect dense content;
- every slide must communicate at a glance.

The deck should therefore be linear enough for stage delivery but modular enough that a presenter can jump to proof during science-fair questioning.

## Exact judging criteria

1. **Solution Effectiveness and Prototype Readiness**  
   How well the prototype addresses the selected challenge and demonstrates core functionality.

2. **Creativity and Innovation**  
   How original and innovative the approach is compared with existing solutions.

3. **Scope and Effort**  
   How appropriate and substantial the work is considering the hackathon timeframe.

4. **Technical Execution and Feasibility**  
   How technically sound, well explained and feasible the solution is using available resources and technologies.

5. **Communication and Presentation**  
   How clearly and convincingly the team communicates its solution, decisions and potential impact.

6. **Open-Source Alignment**  
   How effectively the solution uses open technologies and enables others to access, reuse or adapt the work.

7. **Sustainability and SDG Alignment**  
   How strongly the solution supports environmental sustainability and relevant UN Sustainable Development Goals.

## Additional official "How to Win Reboot 2026" framing

Opening Ceremony material emphasizes:
- responsible AI;
- relevance to societal challenges;
- open source and Digital Public Good direction;
- scalability and real-world impact.

### Implication

The presentation should not merely enumerate features. It needs visible evidence for:
- why this problem matters;
- why this is more than a basic sensor alarm/dashboard;
- what actually runs today;
- why the technical choices are credible;
- what is open/reusable;
- what environmental outcome the system is intended to influence;
- what remains unvalidated.

## Challenge 2 framing

Official Challenge 2 asks for an AI-powered tool combining some combination of:
- sensor readings;
- product information;
- location and time;
- transportation/storage records;

to:
- detect cold-chain issues;
- estimate spoilage risk;
- predict remaining shelf life;
- provide timely alerts;
- recommend practical actions such as inspect/reroute, adjust storage, prioritize products or redirect surplus when safety allows.

### Implication

The deck should make the chain from **data → intelligence → operational decision → food-loss relevance** obvious.

A slide sequence that stops at "we monitor temperature" under-explains the challenge fit.

## Open-source and DPG rules

Official material requires/openly emphasizes:
- open source;
- open data/open technologies;
- proper attribution;
- code quality/documentation;
- reusable/adaptable work;
- alignment with Digital Public Goods / Digital Principles;
- recognized licensing for challenge inputs/outputs.

### Implication

Open source should not be a tiny logo cemetery on the last slide. The presentation should show one concise piece of evidence that the solution is reusable: architecture, repo/license, open model/data provenance, documented interface, reproducible demo path, or equivalent.

## Rules relevant to truthfulness

- Projects are developed during the hackathon.
- Pre-existing IP/components can be used with disclosure/crediting.
- Teams retain their IP.
- Final work should be the team's own submission.

### Presentation implication

Do not blur:
- pre-existing/open-source component;
- reused scientific dataset/model method;
- hackathon-built work;
- future roadmap.

That distinction increases credibility rather than weakening the pitch.
